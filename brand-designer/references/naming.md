# Company and product naming

Use when creating a name or explicitly authorized to rename. Preserve an established name unless renaming is in scope. A supplied name can be provisional: determine its status from the brief.

## Brief and candidates

Build on the approved strategy. Establish what is being named (company, product, service, or family), target markets and languages, desired associations, practical spelling/pronunciation constraints, naming architecture, and words or associations to avoid. Ask only for missing information that changes the decision.

Explore distinct naming territories, then present a manageable shortlist, normally three to five candidates, with:

- Name, pronunciation and spelling; intended meaning and derivation. Separate coined storytelling from verified etymology.
- Connection to positioning, audience, personality, and future expansion.
- Memorability, differentiation, spoken/dictation usability, and relevant language or cultural concerns.
- Relationship to the parent brand and possible product-family names when needed.
- Known conflicts, screening evidence, uncertainties, and recommendation with tradeoffs.

Do not choose by a numerical score alone. Do not invent linguistic findings or customer validation. Browse authoritative sources for current domain observations or preliminary name-conflict checks when those checks are requested or material; record query scope, market, date, and sources. An untested name is untested, and a domain observation is not proof of availability to register or trademark clearance.

## Name gate and documentation

Present a concrete recommendation and obtain selection before investing in the final name-dependent identity. Existing explicit delegation can authorize selection; record its scope. Do not repeat the gate when the supplied name is already approved. Research and non-dependent work may continue while approval is pending.

Record the selected name, capitalization, accents, pronunciation, approved rationale, descriptors/tagline, and family rules in the business record and manual. Rejected candidates belong in working records, not the final approved manual unless requested. Name acceptance does not authorize domain purchase or trademark filing.
