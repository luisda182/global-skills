# Live research

Use this before defining a benchmark, and refresh consequential claims before approving an implementation that depends on them.

## Selection and evidence

Identify direct competitors plus a relevant adjacent workflow or manual substitute. Usually two or three strong comparators are enough; explain narrow coverage. Match audience, platform, region, business model, and product version. Prioritize official product pages, help, release notes, public demos, and specifications. Use user reports as discovery signals, not prevalence estimates or proof of internal architecture.

For every adopted claim record source ID, title, direct URL, publisher, access date, page/version date when available, observation, scope limitations, confidence, and requirement IDs. Open sources supporting consequential claims; search snippets alone are provisional. Record inaccessible sources as inaccessible. Never invent citations or infer “absent” from missing documentation.

Use a feature comparison to identify expected workflows, differentiation hypotheses, and exclusions. Do not equate competitor popularity with user demand. Validate user needs separately. Record contradictions explicitly; choose a conservative interpretation or defer the disputed detail. Pricing, free-tier limits, legal rules, and release-dependent behavior need a fresh check when material.

## From observation to requirement

Example: a competitor documents chapter rooms → observed workflow organization. Proposed requirement: previews respect the reader's selected spoiler boundary. That enforcement is our design proposal, not proof about the competitor.

Specify whether each benchmark item is user-required, competitor-observed, risk-derived, or exploratory. Cite the source for observations and state the rationale for proposals. Do not copy protected content, branding, private datasets, or nonpublic implementation details. Review content/data licensing before reuse.

## Stop conditions

When browsing is unavailable, deliver a provisional research log and unresolved questions. Do not quietly substitute memory for live research. Continue independent discovery and planning; withhold unsupported “competitive level 4” claims. Refresh evidence after material category, region, or scope changes.
